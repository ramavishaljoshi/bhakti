What is a Naam Jaap counter?

A Naam Jaap counter is a small digital tool that helps you keep track of how many times you have repeated a name of the Divine or a mantra during your daily practice. Where a traditional japa mala uses 108 beads strung on a thread, a digital jaap counter replaces those beads with a screen you can tap. The intention is the same: free the mind from counting so it can rest on the sound.

This tool is built for people who do Ram Naam, Radhe Radhe, Hare Krishna, Om Namah Shivaya, the Gayatri Mantra, Waheguru, or any personal mantra. It works as a clean jaap counter online, a japa mala counter, and a mantra counter for 108 rounds — all in one private page.

Why 108 counts in one mala?

The number 108 appears in Hindu, Buddhist, Sikh and Jain traditions. It is considered the number of completion. A few reasons it shows up so often:

  • 1, 0, 8 are read as one thing, nothing, everything — the whole spectrum of reality.
  • It equals 11 × 22 × 33, which yoga and tantra texts use as a symbol of inner structure.
  • Classical Indian medicine describes 108 subtle channels (nadis) converging at the heart.
  • The Sanskrit alphabet has 54 letters, each with a masculine and feminine form — 54 × 2 = 108.

You don't have to believe any of this for the practice to work. What matters is that 108 is a complete round, and the rhythm of finishing one full round, pausing, and starting again is the heartbeat of japa.

How to use this free japa counter

  1. Pick a mantra from the panel, or type your own in the Tweaks tray.
  2. Tap anywhere on the ring — or press Space — once per repetition.
  3. The ring fills, the count rises. At 108, the round auto-saves and the counter rolls to the next mala.
  4. Use Undo if you double-tap, or Reset to start the current mala over.
  5. Open Export to download your history as CSV, or copy JSON.

Digital mala vs physical mala

Physical mala tradition

Tactile, no battery, no screen between you and the practice. Best for long sessions, quiet places, and any setting where holding a thread of beads is part of the discipline.

Digital mala on the move

Counts itself, never loses track, records sessions, works on a walk, in a queue, while travelling. Ideal as a companion to your physical mala, or when carrying one isn't possible.

Many people use both. The physical mala for the morning sitting, the digital mala counter online through the day. This page is designed to make that handoff feel calm.

Privacy and offline saving

This counter is private by default. There is no account, no analytics attached to your chanting, and nothing leaves your device unless you press Export. Every completed mala is written to your browser's local storage as a small record: 

{
  id: "mala_1715600000_3",
  start: "2026-05-13T05:42:11Z",
  end:   "2026-05-13T05:48:36Z",
  count: 108,
  size:  108,
  durationSec: 385,
  mantra: "Radhe Radhe",
  deity:  "Radhe",

Saves happen the instant a round completes — never mid-count, so the tap never lags. If you ever cleared this device's browser data, the history is gone with it. Use Export → CSV once a week if you want a backup.
